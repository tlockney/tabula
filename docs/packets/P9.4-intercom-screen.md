# P9.4 — Intercom screen and ringing UI

Side: build   Tier: standard   Depends on: P9.3
Verification: hardware
Issue: #142
Status: awaiting-hardware

## Why

A call needs a person-facing surface: choose who to call, see an incoming call, answer or decline, see who is connected for the whole call, and hang up. ADR 0025 decision 6 describes the screen; ADR 0028 requires that the screen always shows who is connected.

## Do

1. An intercom screen: lists the other intercom-capable devices by HA area name (device name when no area is set), plus a Broadcast button. The device list comes from HA through the existing channel; add the minimal TLP or settings plumbing only if none exists, and record it in the Result. Devices in the caller's own area are not listed for two-way calls (ADR 0025).
2. Incoming ring UI: full-screen over the dashboard and over a screensaver, wakes the panel, shows caller name and mode, Answer and Decline, stops at the ring limit. A broadcast receiver shows who is speaking and a Reply button that places a two-way call back.
3. In-call UI: always shows who is connected, call duration and remaining time to the hard limit, a Hang up button, and "cannot talk back" on a listen-only device. The banner stays while a call is up, including over a screensaver.
4. Action registry: actions to open the intercom screen and to call a fixed target (device or area) so gestures, the side menu and dashboard links can use them (ADR 0025 decision 6).
5. Compose or view tests against the fake engine: every state renders, buttons call the right engine methods, the banner persists, listen-only wording. Screenshots of each state from the desk or an emulator without audio are welcome in the Result.
6. `docs/provenance/P9.4.md`: independent design.

## Result

Status: awaiting-hardware. CI part only: nothing here was run on a device or an emulator, no audio was played or captured, no `tools/device/*` script was run and `TABULA_AUDIO_OK` was never set. No `PeerConnection` was opened anywhere: the screen's tests use P9.3's fake engine, and the directory's tests use a fake `HaChannel`.

### What landed

1. **The screen.** `IntercomUi` (`:core`, `net.lockney.tabula.core.intercom`) is the whole brain: it renders one `IntercomScreen` (Closed, Directory, Ringing, InCall), takes every `IntercomAction`, and formats the duration and the time left against `Constants.INTERCOM_CALL_LIMIT_S`. It holds no Android, no timer and no connection. `IntercomScreenView` (`:app`) draws it with plain framework views (ADR 0008) as the activity's last overlay, so it covers the dashboard, the photo slideshow and the screensaver.
2. **The device list.** HA answers it over the tablet's own WebSocket connection, next to TLP (ADR 0017): a new `tabula/intercom_directory` command in `integration/custom_components/tabula/intercom_directory.py`, resolved from the connection's user exactly as `tabula/register` does, refused for a connection that is not a paired tablet (`unknown_device`) or that lacks `intercom_listen` (`not_supported`). Each entry carries the HA device registry id (`target_device_id`), the device name, its area id and name, `can_talk` and `in_call`. Same-area tablets and the tablet itself are left out, matching `intercom._select_targets`. **This is not a TLP frame**: the protocol's frames are exactly as P9.1 merged them, so P9.1b (#173) is free to reshape them. P9.1's Result item 10 named this dependency for P9.4; the packet's "add the minimal TLP or settings plumbing only if none exists" is answered with the existing socket and no schema or settings change.
3. **Ringing.** A ring renders `Ringing` with the caller, the countdown to `INTERCOM_RING_LIMIT_S`, Answer and Decline; `IntercomManager.ring` now calls an `onRing` hook and the service wakes the panel with `ScreenManager.command(true, null)` (F-SCR-14). It deliberately does not dismiss the screensaver: a call at night draws over the dark screen instead of lighting the dashboard. A ring that nobody answers ends at the ring limit inside the engine and the screen falls back to whatever it was showing.
4. **In-call.** The banner stays for the call's whole life, over the screensaver included: who is connected, `m:ss` duration, and the time left to the hard limit. A call this device started reads **Calling** until the other side answers and **In call** after it: P9.3 decision 9 puts a caller in `in_call` from its own start, so the state cannot tell the two apart, and `CallInfo` now carries an `answered` bit the engine sets from the callee's relayed answer, from this device's own answer and from ICE connecting (review of #176). The banner is a banner: a call that is up draws as a strip at the top of the screen over whatever is showing, not as a full-screen sheet, so the dashboard and the screensaver behind it stay visible and usable. A listen-only device says "This tablet cannot talk back" and still answers. A broadcast's receiver sees who is speaking with Reply (which ends the broadcast and then starts the two-way call back once the engine is idle, because a device is in one call at a time) and Stop listening; a broadcast's sender sees **Broadcasting** with Hang up.
5. **Actions (ADR 0025 decision 6).** `DashboardLinks` gains `tabula://intercom`, `tabula://intercom-call/<ha device id>`, `tabula://intercom-area/<area id>` and `tabula://intercom-broadcast`; a target action with no id, two segments or a character outside `[A-Za-z0-9_-]` is refused with a reason (F-DSH-09). The service carries each out: a fixed target fetches the directory first so the call screen shows the same label the list would. F-GST-07's full action registry does not exist yet (its `TABULA_ACTIONS` comment says the registry arrives with Phase 4), so these are the actions the existing link surface runs; the side menu is HA's frontend sidebar and is not ours to extend - the links are the reachable path today, and gestures/menu entries become available when the registry lands.
6. **Provenance.** `docs/provenance/P9.4.md`, fifteen rows, all independent design with the ADRs they follow.

### Decisions taken here (in the packet's gift)

1. **The directory is an HA WebSocket command, not a TLP frame.** The packet allows "the minimal TLP or settings plumbing"; a command on the socket the device already holds (ADR 0017) adds no frame, no schema and no protocol revision, and P9.1 Result item 10 already made the directory P9.4's dependency. The command is deliberately not admin-only like `tabula/panel_*`: it answers from the connection's own user.
2. **Labels are HA's area name, device name when absent**, computed in the app from `area_name ?: name`, and the list order is HA's (label, then name). Same-area tablets are omitted rather than shown disabled, because the integration refuses that call and skips that broadcast.
3. **A caller reads "Calling" until answered.** P9.3 decision 9 reports a caller's `in_call` from its own start; the screen shows the direction's word instead of claiming a connection, and switches to "In call" on the ringed device's answer.
4. **Reply ends the broadcast, then calls back** when the engine reports idle. A device is in one call at a time (PROTOCOL 4.5), and starting the call back inside the broadcast would be refused `busy` by the device itself. No timer and no new constant: the engine's own state change is the trigger.
5. **One refresh a second, only while a call is up.** The service publishes the screen from the engine's flows, from every action and from that tick; an idle tablet publishes nothing. No wake lock of its own is taken for this: the call holds one (P9.3).
6. **No ring tone.** P9.3's Result said the ring tone would belong to this packet; the packet's own brief does not ask for one and the owner's audio rule forbids playing anything in a test or run. The screen is visual only, and ADR 0025 decision 1's tone before auto-answer stays with P9.5.
7. **A tap on the call UI is not idle.** `MainActivity.onUserInteraction` ignores a touch while the overlay shows, as it does for a notification card, so answering a call over a screensaver leaves the screensaver where it was.

### Gaps and deviations

1. **An HA-started call still cannot reach its own caller's engine** (P9.2 open question 1, restated in P9.3's deviation 1). It does not block this packet: every call the screen starts is device-started, so the id is the device's and the offer leaves from the device. What a person sees for an HA-started call is the callee's ring; the caller's screen stays on the dashboard. Implemented as merged PROTOCOL.md describes; no protocol change was made.
   **Decided, not by this packet:** ADR 0037 section 2 settles it with `intercom_start` (`call_id`, `mode`, `peers` of `{device_id, name}`), gated by a new `intercom_caller` capability, and P9.1b (#173) implements it on both sides. The screen needs no change when it lands: it draws whatever the engine reports, so a prepared caller is an ordinary outgoing call whose `CallInfo.direction` is OUTGOING and whose `peerName` is the name HA sent. That is why this packet kept to v1 frames rather than anticipating the command.
2. **A stale `intercom_state` that HA has no call for** (P9.2 open question 2) is still the device guards' answer, as the protocol and P9.3's Result say: the call ends at its own limit or at the disconnect grace, and the next registration reports idle. This packet adds no clearing path and no new state; the screen simply renders whatever the engine reports, so a stale state shows as a call the person can hang up - which is the honest picture.
3. **A broadcast has no signaling path in the merged integration** (P9.3 deviation 2). The Reply button is implemented and tested against the engine, but with `_async_signal`'s broadcast early return a reply's offer never crosses the relay, so the call back would ring and carry nothing. **Decided:** ADR 0037 sections 1, 3, 4 and 7 make a broadcast one call with one peer connection per receiver, add the optional `peer_device_id` to `intercom_signal`, remove the broadcast exception and bound the fan-out at `INTERCOM_BROADCAST_MAX_TARGETS`; P9.1b (#173) implements all of it. Nothing here assumed either shape: the sender's screen only says "Broadcasting", and a receiver's Reply is an ordinary two-way call to one device, which is a peer connection the star already has.
4. **`INTERCOM_CALL_LIMIT_S` is used through `Constants` at its provisional 300 s.** No code here hard-codes it; the screen shows "5:00 left" from the constant, so a change in ADR 0002 moves the screen with the engine.
5. **The rendering tests are JVM tests in `:core`, not Robolectric view tests.** The packet asks for "Compose or view tests against the fake engine"; every decision those would assert - which state draws what, which button asks the engine for what, the banner staying up, the listen-only wording - lives in `IntercomUi` and is tested there against `FakeCallEngine`, 30 cases. `IntercomScreenView` only maps fields to `TextView`s and `Button`s, with no branch the model does not already decide, so a Robolectric test would restate the model's assertions through a heavier harness. If a later packet adds logic to the view, that is where a view test belongs.
6. **No screenshots.** The packet welcomes them from the desk or an emulator; the owner's rule for this packet forbids an emulator with audio and any device run, so there is none. The view is plain views with no measurement logic, and the model it draws is fully covered by the tests above.
7. **The full action registry (F-GST-07) is not built yet.** The four intercom actions live in the existing `tabula://` link surface; when the registry lands they become ordinary registry entries.

### Review (PR #176) fixes

Two reviews landed at `68032f1`: an engine/UI walk-through and a wiring review. Every blocking, should-fix and low item is addressed here; nothing about the protocol, the constants or the guards changed.

1. **The screen could not be opened** (blocking, wiring review). `observeIntercom` published and fetched on `OpenDirectory` but never called `perform`, so `directoryOpen` stayed false, `render` returned `Closed` and the sheet never appeared - and a failed fetch rendered `Closed` too, hiding the "HA is not reachable" line. Opening now performs the action first (the sheet is up at once), then fetches and publishes; a failure leaves the note on screen. The service's action mapping is now a named function, `handleIntercomAction`, so the branch is not buried in a lambda.
2. **A broadcast reply addressed the wrong id** (blocking, wiring review). A ring names its caller by TLP id (`intercom_ring.caller_device_id`) while `intercom_start_request.target_device_id` is an HA device registry id, so the reply started a call HA could not resolve and the device sat in an outgoing call until its no-answer timer. The directory entry now carries the tablet's TLP id (`tabula_device_id`) beside the HA one, the app keeps the pair, and `reply()` resolves one to the other. A sender the list does not know gets "That tablet is not on the list, so it cannot be called back" and no call is started. The list is also fetched when a call arrives, so the pair is there before anyone taps Reply. Two new tests cover both outcomes.
3. **"Calling" lasted the whole call** (must fix, first review). `CallInfo` gains `answered`, set by the engine when the callee's relayed answer arrives, when this device answers, and when ICE connects; `titleOf` reads it instead of inferring from `direction`. `FakeCallEngine` reports it too. Three new `CallMachineTest` cases and one `IntercomUiTest` case cover it.
4. **A listen-only tablet offered buttons it could not honour** (must fix, first review). `IntercomUi` puts `NO_MICROPHONE` on the directory whenever this device cannot capture, and the view disables every peer button in that state, so the reason is on screen before a tap. `test_a_tablet_with_no_microphone_offers_no_call_and_says_why` now asserts the note, not only the flag.
5. **A fetch could hold up every other button** (should fix). Every press now runs in its own coroutine, so an eight-second directory fetch cannot delay Hang up or Decline, and opening no longer blocks on the fetch either.
6. **`IntercomUi`'s state was not confined** (should fix). Every entry point is `@Synchronized`, the same guard `CallMachine` uses, since the service calls it from three coroutines.
7. **Lows.** A pending reply carries the call it waited for and is dropped if a different call becomes the call in between; peers carry `canTalk` into the list ("— listens only") and a busy one says "— in a call"; hang-up goes through `IntercomManager.hangUp(reason)`, so it is logged like every other end, and that method now takes the reason; `SHEET`'s comment says 95 percent instead of "opaque"; the double blank line in the service's companion is gone; the duplicate item 6 in this Result is item 7.
8. **The banner is compact** (non-blocking, wiring review). A call that is up draws as a top strip with a translucent background and the overlay stops taking clicks, so the dashboard and the screensaver behind it stay visible and usable - which is what packet item 3, the provenance row and device step 7 describe. The list and the ring stay full-screen, because they ask for an answer.

### Second review (PR #176)

One blocking item at `2aa62a3`, and it is the constants rule: `IntercomDirectory.TIMEOUT_MS = 8_000` and `TabulaService.INTERCOM_TICK_MS = 1_000` were values invented in code, and AGENTS.md reserves timeouts and refresh intervals to ADR 0002. Both are gone:

1. **The directory's wait is `COMMAND_TIMEOUT`.** ADR 0002 already records 10 s for "HA has to answer one command"; the directory request *is* one, so `Constants.COMMAND_TIMEOUT_S` (new, mirroring the recorded value) is what the fetch waits on, and `IntercomDirectory.timeoutMs` derives from it. No new number was chosen for this screen.
2. **The call screen's refresh interval is `INTERCOM_UI_TICK_MS`.** It is recorded in ADR 0002's intercom section at 1000 ms, with why: the duration and the time left are `m:ss`, so a second is the finest step that changes either, and an idle screen publishes nothing. The service reads the constant; its local `INTERCOM_TICK_MS` is deleted.
3. **Drift guards.** `IntercomConstantsTest` asserts both recorded values, and `IntercomDirectoryTest` asserts the fetch's timeout is `COMMAND_TIMEOUT_S * 1000`, so neither can drift back into a literal without a failing test that names the decision.
