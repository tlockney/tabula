# P9.1b — Intercom: the caller's own frame and per-peer signaling

Side: build   Tier: deep   Depends on: P9.1, P9.2, P9.3 (PR #171)
Verification: ci
Issue: #173
Status: awaiting-hardware

## Why

Two protocol gaps were recorded by the merged packets: P9.2's open question 1 (an HA-started call never reaches its own caller's engine, so it cannot open the call or match an end to it) and P9.3's deviations 1 and 2 (the same gap from the device side, and a broadcast whose signaling the integration's relay drops, with no way for a sender with several receivers to tell them apart). ADR 0037 decides the smallest additive fix: one command that tells a device it is the caller (`intercom_start`, gated by the new capability `intercom_caller`), one optional `peer_device_id` on `intercom_signal` in the caller's direction only, and a relay that is a star by peer instead of a fan-out by call.

This packet implements that on both sides — schema, fixtures, PROTOCOL, the mock device, the integration's relay and services, the Kotlin codec and the app's intercom wiring — so the rest of Phase 9 (P9.4 and P9.5 for the screen and auto-answer, P9.6 for voice) runs against a complete signaling path. CI only: nothing here needs a device, and a broadcast's audio and cost stay unmeasured until the owner schedules a run.

### Deviations

1. **`PrepareOutcome` replaces "reuse `StartOutcome`"** (the brief said a sealed result "as `start`
   has"). `StartOutcome` carries `CannotTalk` and `InvalidTarget`, which cannot happen for a frame
   HA only sends after `_check_caller`; a three-member `PrepareOutcome` says what a device can
   actually answer.
2. **The missing-`intercom_talk` early return in `_async_start_request` also ends the call.** The
   brief lists eight refusals; ADR 0037 decision 10's rule ("every refusal on the device-event
   path ends what the device already started") covers this one too, and a device that sent the
   request is in the call it minted. Sending `intercom_end` to a device not in it is `ok` and
   changes nothing, so the extra end is safe.
3. **A caller's signals are labelled by the call's peer list, not by the connections open.** The
   first draft labelled by `connections.size`, which is 1 while the second connection is still
   being created in `prepare`, so the first offer of a broadcast went out unlabelled. The machine
   keeps the resolved `peerIds` and labels from that (`prepare adopts the call and opens one
   connection per peer` fails without it).
4. **`intercom_caller` is declared unconditionally by the app.** A build that declares it is
   always able to be prepared; the microphone grant gates `intercom_talk` alone, so the caller
   capability does not follow it. `the capabilities follow the microphone grant` asserts the new
   list.
5. **A caller that cannot capture is prepared `RECEIVE_ONLY`.** HA's `_check_caller` refuses such a
   caller before the frame, so this is defence in depth rather than a path a household reaches.

### Decisions taken in the packet's gift

- The `intercom_start` description in the schema, the PROTOCOL wording and the exception messages
  are mine, from ADR 0037's decisions; the ADR's wording is followed clause by clause.
- A `peer_device_id` on a callee's frame is ignored rather than refused (`_async_route_signal`):
  the frame still says which call it is for, and the authenticated sender decides.
- The mock refuses to *send* an unlabelled signal when it is a caller with several peers, which is
  the device side of "every signal the caller sends must carry it".

### Open questions for the architect

1. **Stale `intercom_state` with no call id (P9.2 open question 2, P9.3 deviation 3) is still
   open**, and this packet does not close it: `intercom_end` still names a call id, so a tablet
   that was in a call when HA restarted keeps reporting it until its own hard limit ends it. The
   merged PROTOCOL is what was implemented (ADR 0037 decision 3 gives `peer_device_id` only to
   `intercom_signal`), and the protocol was not changed. **Proposal for the architect:** either
   (a) declare the device guards the whole answer — the boot guard plus the hard limit already
   clear it within `INTERCOM_CALL_LIMIT_S` — and say so in PROTOCOL 4.5's `intercom_end` paragraph,
   or (b) an additive `intercom_end` form that omits `call_id` and means "whatever you are in",
   gated by a new capability so a v1 device is never sent one. This packet is not blocked either
   way; only the wording of a future ADR is.
2. **The cost of a broadcast is still unmeasured, so the cap of 8 stays provisional** (ADR 0037
   decision 7). A hardware run that broadcasts to two receivers and records the sender's CPU and
   the audio quality is what confirms or changes it; that run is the owner's to schedule.
3. **The device part of P9.3 (a real call between two tablets) is still unrun**, and this packet
   unblocks it: a call HA starts and a broadcast of two receivers are now the two paths worth
   measuring. The steps are P9.3's device part; nothing in this packet's own list needs a device.
