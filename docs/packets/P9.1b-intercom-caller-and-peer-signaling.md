# P9.1b — Intercom: the caller's own frame and per-peer signaling

Side: build   Tier: deep   Depends on: P9.1, P9.2, P9.3
Verification: ci
Issue: #173
Status: ready

## Why

Two protocol gaps were recorded by the merged packets: P9.2's open question 1 (an HA-started call never reaches its own caller's engine, so it cannot open the call or match an end to it) and P9.3's deviations 1 and 2 (the same gap from the device side, and a broadcast whose signaling the integration's relay drops, with no way for a sender with several receivers to tell them apart). ADR 0037 decides the smallest additive fix: one command that tells a device it is the caller (`intercom_start`, gated by the new capability `intercom_caller`), one optional `peer_device_id` on `intercom_signal` in the caller's direction only, and a relay that is a star by peer instead of a fan-out by call.

This packet implements that on both sides — schema, fixtures, PROTOCOL, the mock device, the integration's relay and services, the Kotlin codec and the app's intercom wiring — so the rest of Phase 9 (P9.4 and P9.5 for the screen and auto-answer, P9.6 for voice) runs against a complete signaling path. CI only: nothing here needs a device, and a broadcast's audio and cost stay unmeasured until the owner schedules a run.

## Do

1. **Schema (`schema/tlp-v1.schema.json`).**
   - Add `intercom_caller` to `$defs/capability` (single segment; PROTOCOL 4.0, 8).
   - Add `$defs/intercom_peer`: an object with `device_id` (`#/$defs/device_id`) and `name` (1–64 characters), both required, `additionalProperties: false`.
   - Add `$defs/command_intercom_start`, shaped like the other command frames (`id`, `type: "event"`, `event.command_id`, `event.command: "intercom_start"`), with `args`: `call_id` (`#/$defs/intercom_call_id`), `mode` (`#/$defs/intercom_mode`) and `peers` (array of `#/$defs/intercom_peer`, `minItems` 1, `maxItems: 8`). Exactly one peer when `mode` is `call` — the same `allOf`/`if`/`then` style `event_intercom_start_request` already uses for its target rule. Add it to `x-tabula-frames`.
   - Add an optional `peer_device_id` (`#/$defs/device_id`) to `command_intercom_signal.args` and to the `intercom_signal` block in `$defs/event.allOf`. Both descriptions say what ADR 0037 decision 3 says: it names the other end of that one connection, it is present only in a caller's frames of a call with more than two devices, and a receiver never sees or sends it.

2. **Fixtures (`schema/fixtures/tlp/v1/`).** Valid: `command_intercom_start.json` (mode `call`, one peer), `command_intercom_start.broadcast.json` (mode `broadcast`, two peers), `command_intercom_signal.peer.json` (an offer from a broadcast caller naming a receiver), `event.intercom_signal.peer.json`. 5. **Mock device (`mockdevice/src/mockdevice/device.py`, `testserver.py`).**
   - Declare `intercom_caller`; add `"intercom_start": "intercom_caller"` to `COMMAND_CAPABILITIES`.
   - `_cmd_intercom_start(args)` (new): adopt or merge the call — the id, mode, peers and role. A device with a different call up answers `busy`; one already in this call keeps it and reconciles its peer list (a peer it lacks is added, one the list no longer names is dropped). A `call` goes to `in_call` and arms the hard limit from now; a `broadcast` goes to `broadcasting` and arms the limit from now. Record the peers so a test can assert them.

6. **Integration (`integration/custom_components/tabula/`).**
   - `const.py`: `CAP_CALLER = "intercom_caller"`; `"intercom_start": frozenset({CAP_CALLER})` in `COMMAND_CAPABILITIES`; `INTERCOM_BROADCAST_MAX_TARGETS`. `Command` names gain `"intercom_start"`.
   - `intercom.py`:
     - `async_start_call` gains the capability decision ADR 0037 decision 8 states: the caller frame is needed when the service started the call or when there is more than one target; a caller without `CAP_CALLER` then raises `intercom_caller_unsupported`. A device-started call with one target by such a device still runs, with no `intercom_start`.
     - Order: `_select_targets`, ring, `_settle_rings`, then `intercom_start` to the caller with the settled peer list (`[{device_id, name}]`, name from the same label as `caller_name`, capped at 64 characters). A NACK to it ends the call (`busy` for a `busy` NACK, `error` otherwise) and raises. Only then does the call become ready.
     - Holding: while a call is not yet ready, a caller's `intercom_signal` is queued on the call record instead of relayed, and flushed when the call becomes ready or dropped when it ends. The queue is bounded by the ring limit already running; do not add a timer.
     - `_async_signal`: delete the `MODE_BROADCAST` early return. Pick the recipient as ADR 0037 decision 4 says: a caller with more than two devices relays only to the `peer_device_id` its event names (a missing or unknown peer is logged and dropped, never relayed and never ending the call); a caller of a two-device call relays to the other device; a callee's signal goes to the caller alone, with `peer_device_id` set to the sender when the caller declared `CAP_CALLER` and the call has more than two devices. Keep the existing `STATE_CONNECTING` handling for two-way calls.
     - `_select_targets`: a broadcast (either path) whose free target set exceeds `INTERCOM_BROADCAST_MAX_TARGETS` raises `intercom_broadcast_too_many` naming the devices. Never trim.
     - `_async_start_request`: a device-started broadcast or area call goes through the same code, so the requesting device receives `intercom_start` too; its own id is not in the peer list it gets back.
     - `strings.json` and `translations/en.json`: `intercom_caller_unsupported` (the tablet's app needs updating) and `intercom_broadcast_too_many`.
   - Tests (`integration/tests/test_tabula_intercom.py`): an HA-started call prepares the caller with the callee and the minted id, and the call ends on that id; a caller's offer sent before the ring is held and flushed after `intercom_start`, never dropped; a broadcast to two receivers rings both, sends each its own offer, and routes each receiver's answer to the caller only (no receiver sees another's signal); a two-device call's frames carry no `peer_device_id`; a signal naming an unknown peer is dropped and the call survives; a broadcast of nine targets raises and rings nobody; an HA-started call with a caller that does not declare `intercom_caller` raises and rings nobody; a device-started two-way call by that same caller still works; a broadcast receiver without `intercom_caller` still hears the caller's offer; a `busy` or `error` NACK to `intercom_start` ends the call before anything is relayed; the call limit still ends a broadcast and every receiver gets `intercom_end`.
