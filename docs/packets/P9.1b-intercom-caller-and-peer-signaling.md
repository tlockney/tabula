# P9.1b — Intercom: the caller's own frame and per-peer signaling

Side: build   Tier: deep   Depends on: P9.1, P9.2, P9.3 (PR #171)
Verification: ci
Issue: #173
Status: ready once P9.3 (#141, PR #171) merges — the Result of P9.3 that this brief builds on is only on that branch

## Why

Two protocol gaps were recorded by the merged packets: P9.2's open question 1 (an HA-started call never reaches its own caller's engine, so it cannot open the call or match an end to it) and P9.3's deviations 1 and 2 (the same gap from the device side, and a broadcast whose signaling the integration's relay drops, with no way for a sender with several receivers to tell them apart). ADR 0037 decides the smallest additive fix: one command that tells a device it is the caller (`intercom_start`, gated by the new capability `intercom_caller`), one optional `peer_device_id` on `intercom_signal` in the caller's direction only, and a relay that is a star by peer instead of a fan-out by call.

This packet implements that on both sides — schema, fixtures, PROTOCOL, the mock device, the integration's relay and services, the Kotlin codec and the app's intercom wiring — so the rest of Phase 9 (P9.4 and P9.5 for the screen and auto-answer, P9.6 for voice) runs against a complete signaling path. CI only: nothing here needs a device, and a broadcast's audio and cost stay unmeasured until the owner schedules a run.

6. **Integration (`integration/custom_components/tabula/`).**
   - `const.py`: `CAP_CALLER = "intercom_caller"`; `"intercom_start": frozenset({CAP_CALLER})` in `COMMAND_CAPABILITIES`; `INTERCOM_BROADCAST_MAX_TARGETS`. `Command` names gain `"intercom_start"`.
   - `intercom.py`:
     - `_check_caller`: a real conjunction, not the any-of gate. A caller of any call declares `intercom_listen` and `intercom_talk`; a caller of a broadcast therefore needs both (today it needs talk only). A caller that cannot receive `intercom_signal` has no return path — `async_command` would raise `command_unsupported` into a log line and the broadcast would be silent. Do not widen `COMMAND_CAPABILITIES` instead: it is an any-of test, so `frozenset({CAP_CALLER, CAP_LISTEN})` would loosen the gate.
     - Order: `_select_targets`, ring, `_settle_rings`, then `intercom_start` to the caller with the settled peer list (`[{device_id, name}]`, name from the same label as `caller_name`, capped at 64 characters). A NACK to it ends the call and raises: `intercom_busy` for a `busy` NACK (the existing key), `intercom_start_failed` for any other, with the device's label as its placeholder. Only then does the call become ready.
     - Holding: while a call is not yet ready, a caller's `intercom_signal` is queued on the call record instead of relayed, and flushed when the call becomes ready or dropped when it ends. The queue is bounded by the `COMMAND_TIMEOUT` on the `intercom_start` result, not by a timer of its own — a broadcast has no ring timer running, and a two-way call's ring limit is the looser bound. Do not add a timer.
     - `_async_signal`: delete the `MODE_BROADCAST` early return. Pick the recipient as ADR 0037 decision 4 says: a caller with more than two devices relays only to the `peer_device_id` its event names (a missing or unknown peer is logged and dropped, never relayed and never ending the call); a caller of a two-device call relays to the other device; a callee's signal goes to the caller alone, with `peer_device_id` set to the sender when the caller declared `CAP_CALLER` and the call has more than two devices. Keep the existing `STATE_CONNECTING` handling for two-way calls.
     - `_select_targets`: a broadcast (either path) whose free target set exceeds `INTERCOM_BROADCAST_MAX_TARGETS` raises `intercom_broadcast_too_many` naming the devices. Never trim.
     - The caller-frame capability decision ADR 0037 decision 8 states: the frame is needed when the service started the call or when there is more than one target; a caller without `CAP_CALLER` then raises `intercom_caller_unsupported`. A device-started call with one target by such a device still runs, with no `intercom_start`.
     - `_async_start_request`: a device-started broadcast or area call goes through the same code, so the requesting device receives `intercom_start` too; its own id is not in the peer list it gets back.
     - A refusal on the device-event path ends what the device started, and that is all seven of them (ADR 0037 decision 10), not only the two this ADR adds: the missing `intercom_caller`, an oversize broadcast, an area resolving to more than one device for a `call`, targets all in the caller's own area, a busy target, a call id already in use, and no capable target. In each case the device has already entered the call it minted (P9.3), so `_async_start_request`'s `except` sends it `intercom_end` reason `command` on that id before logging. An event has no result to NACK, so this end is the only thing that stops the tablet from sitting in `in_call` or `broadcasting` with nothing carrying it.
     - On a callee's signal the integration ignores any `peer_device_id` the frame carries and takes the id from `record.device_id`, so a receiver cannot address a frame to an arbitrary peer (ADR 0037 decision 4).
     - `strings.json` and `translations/en.json`: `intercom_caller_unsupported` (the tablet's app needs updating), `intercom_broadcast_too_many`, and `intercom_start_failed` (the caller could not be prepared, with the device label).
   - Tests (`integration/tests/test_tabula_intercom.py`): an HA-started call prepares the caller with the callee and the minted id, and the call ends on that id; a caller's offer sent before the ring is held and flushed after `intercom_start`, never dropped; a broadcast to two receivers rings both, sends each its own offer, and routes each receiver's answer to the caller only (no receiver sees another's signal); a two-device call's frames carry no `peer_device_id`; a signal naming an unknown peer is dropped and the call survives; a broadcast of nine targets raises and rings nobody; an HA-started call with a caller that does not declare `intercom_caller` raises and rings nobody; a device-started two-way call by that same caller still works; a broadcast receiver without `intercom_caller` still hears the caller's offer; a `busy` or `error` NACK to `intercom_start` ends the call before anything is relayed (the first raising `intercom_busy`, the second `intercom_start_failed`); a broadcast requested by a device without `intercom_caller` gets `intercom_end` reason `command` on its own id and rings nobody; an oversize broadcast, and each of the other five refusals, from that same path gets the same end; a broadcast caller that declares talk and not listen is refused by `_check_caller` rather than prepared into a silent call; the call limit still ends a broadcast and every receiver gets `intercom_end`.

8. **App wiring, on the P9.3 branch.** `CallEngine` gains a `prepare(callId, mode, peers)` entry point (a sealed result, as `start` has), `IntercomControl` gains `start(args: IntercomStartArgs)` and `TabulaCommands` routes the command. The routing change *is* a signature change: `CallMachine.signal(callId, kind, payload)` gains the peer (`signal(callId, kind, payload, peerDeviceId: String?)`), and `CallEngine.signal`, `FakeCallEngine` and `IntercomManager` follow it, so a caller's outgoing signal can name its connection and a received one can be routed. `CallMachine` replaces its single `peer` with a map keyed by device id: a prepared caller opens one connection per peer and offers to each, a callee keeps exactly one, an incoming signal is routed by `peer_device_id` (an unknown peer is ignored and logged), every outgoing signal from a caller with more than one peer carries the peer id, and ending the call closes every connection. `prepare` for a call id the device already holds reconciles the peer list; for no call it adopts the whole thing; it emits no `intercom_start_request` and reports `in_call` or `broadcasting` as `start` does. Unit tests: prepare with two peers, an answer routed by peer, an unknown peer ignored, an end closing both connections, the adopt-and-merge cases, and the TLP wiring for the new command.
